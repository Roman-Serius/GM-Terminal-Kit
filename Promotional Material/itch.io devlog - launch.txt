TITLE: GM Terminal Kit is out: build fake retro computers for your table (or your next game)

Every great sci-fi or hacker scene has that moment: someone leans over a glowing green screen, types a password, and the room goes quiet while it thinks.

GM Terminal Kit lets you hand your players that moment for real.

It's a free (pay-what-you-want) tool for building fake retro computers: files, folders, passwords, email, programs, alarms and glitches. You build the computer in a simple drag-and-drop workspace, click Player mode, and turn the screen around. No install, no account, no code. It's a single file that runs in your browser, even offline.


WHAT YOU CAN BUILD

- Files and folders full of clues, with password locks, hidden files and files that self-destruct after one read
- User accounts with their own mail, including messages that arrive mid-game
- Programs that "run": progress bars, fake hacking tools, decryption sequences
- Events: when players read a file, unlock a folder or type a phrase, new mail appears, a character starts typing to them, or a countdown begins
- Questions with branching answers ("TRUST ME? Y/N")
- 20 sound effects (alarms, modems, dial tones, static) and a robotic speaking voice, all made by the browser, so there are no audio files to manage
- Screen glitch, shake and flash
- 12 looks: 80s green CRT, amber, DOS PC, 80s home computer, military teletype, 90s corporate, cyberpunk and more, and you can switch mid-game ("RED ALERT")
- Animated GIFs that play as ASCII art, for security cameras, radar sweeps and creepy faces


FOR GAME MASTERS

Use it as a prop at the table. Put a laptop in the middle of the table or the TV on the wall, and let the players type.

- Sci-fi campaigns: the ship's computer in Mothership or Alien, a corporate mainframe in Cyberpunk, a Delta Green field laptop
- Mystery and horror: the victim's computer in a murder mystery, a haunted PC for your Halloween one-shot
- Escape rooms and parties: a terminal puzzle that checks the answer for you

It's built for running a live game:

- Ctrl+Shift+1 to 9 fires an event secretly, mid-scene, so the mail arrives exactly when you want it to
- The Activity log shows what your players typed, including their wrong password guesses
- Answer key lists every password and what it unlocks, so you never forget your own puzzle
- Reset session puts everything back between groups


FOR GAME DEVELOPERS

The kit is also a small engine for terminal games. Build your game in the workspace, then click Export for players. You get one HTML file that opens straight into the terminal, with no workspace and no answers visible. Upload it to itch.io as a browser game, or send it to friends.

- Password-protected content is encrypted inside that file, so players can't dig the answers out of the code
- Players' progress saves in their own browser automatically
- Event map draws your whole game as a diagram, so you can spot the puzzle that can't be solved before your players do
- Run now plays any script instantly, so you can test the finale without replaying the whole game

Everything you make is yours. Share it, upload it, sell it. A credit is always appreciated ("Made with GM Terminal Kit by RomanSerius").


SEE IT IN ACTION

The kit comes with Kestrel Station, a short sample game where every step uses a different feature, and a [KIT] line explains what just happened. It's the fastest way to see what the kit can do, and every script in it is there for you to copy.

On the same page you can play Thornfield Terminal for free in your browser: a guest login to a research lab's computer in 1989, a friendly stranger called Pan, and evidence that needs to get out. It was made entirely in the kit.

Curious how it's put together? For $2 you can get the Thornfield source: the full scenario to open in the kit, with a walkthrough and a map of every event.
